# BrightPath Tutor — Product Foundations

BrightPath is intentionally designed as a **learning coach**, not a substitute for a teacher, caregiver, or other trusted adult. Its first release offers short, grade-aware practice in mathematics, literacy, writing, science, and social studies. The skill structure is informed by grade-level organization in widely used U.S. mathematics and English language arts standards, while remaining adaptable to the curriculum chosen by each family or school. [1] [2] [3]

| Product decision | Implementation consequence |
|---|---|
| Human-centered tutoring | The AI explains, asks guiding questions, offers hints, and encourages independent reasoning rather than simply supplying work. |
| Age-appropriate use | Student-facing conversations use short, concrete language and keep a parent-visible safety boundary. |
| Privacy by default | Learner setup needs only a nickname, grade, and interests. Progress stays on the device in the first release. |
| Parent participation | Progress and controls are placed behind an adult verification step, with no student social feed or public profile. |
| Critical thinking | Lessons require an answer and feedback cycle; the tutor will frame suggestions as support rather than unquestionable authority. |

UNESCO’s education guidance calls for data privacy protection, age-appropriate pedagogical design, and a human-centered approach to generative AI. [1] California’s guidance similarly describes intelligent tutoring as step-by-step support with feedback and hints, while emphasizing that AI should enhance rather than replace human judgment and relationships. [2] BrightPath therefore treats automated responses as bounded instructional support and directs children toward a trusted adult when a request falls outside learning help.

The starter learning library is intentionally limited to one age-appropriate activity per subject and grade level so that each interaction can be reviewed, tested, and expanded responsibly. The content model can later grow into a broader curriculum map, additional skills, multilingual support, and teacher-selected standards after adult consent and curriculum requirements are defined.
